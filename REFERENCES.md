## Technical transparency and risk management

Chandra, Bilva, Jesse Dunietz, Kathleen Roberts, Yooyoung Lee, Peter Fontana, and George Awad. *Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content: An Overview of Technical Approaches to Digital Content Transparency.* NIST AI 100-4. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2024; page updated April 8, 2026. [https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-4](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-4)

This report organizes technical approaches around provenance, watermarking, labeling, detection, testing, and auditing. It supports the layered approach used in the analysis and policy.

National Institute of Standards and Technology. *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile.* NIST AI 600-1, 2024. [https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1)

The profile explains how provenance and synthetic-content detection can support risk management when combined with organizational accountability and testing.

Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity. *C2PA and Content Credentials Explainer*, specification version 2.4. Accessed October 1, 2026. [https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html)

The explainer describes signed manifests, hashes, soft and hard bindings, and the limits of provenance. It states that provenance may be incomplete, metadata may be removed, and provenance alone cannot determine whether content is factually true.

## Platform policies

YouTube Help. "Disclosing use of generative AI content." Accessed October 1, 2026. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)

YouTube requires disclosure for realistic content that is meaningfully altered or generated and may apply labels through creator reporting, Content Credentials, or internal detection.

YouTube Help. "Understanding How this content was made disclosures on YouTube." Accessed October 1, 2026. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836)

This page explains how YouTube can carry forward supported Content Credentials and display information about how a video was made.

Meta Transparency Center. "Misinformation." Accessed October 1, 2026. [https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/misinformation/](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/misinformation/)

Meta's policy includes AI-disclosure requirements for certain photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio. It is used as an example of platform-level labeling rather than as a substitute for internal governance.

## Legal and regulatory landscape

Federal Trade Commission. "FTC Announces Impersonation Rule Goes into Effect Today." April 1, 2024. [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-impersonation-rule-goes-effect-today](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-impersonation-rule-goes-effect-today)

The rule addresses materially false impersonation of government entities and businesses in commerce. It illustrates how existing consumer-protection law can reach conduct enabled by synthetic media.

Federal Communications Commission. "FCC Makes AI-Generated Voices in Robocalls Illegal." February 8, 2024. [https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)

The FCC confirmed that AI-generated voices qualify as artificial voices under Telephone Consumer Protection Act restrictions. This is a context-specific rule, not a universal synthetic-media statute.

Federal Trade Commission. "FTC Announces Winners of Voice Cloning Challenge." April 8, 2024. [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-winners-voice-cloning-challenge](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-winners-voice-cloning-challenge)

The challenge highlights authentication, interference, and detection proposals while emphasizing that no single solution resolves voice-cloning risk.

## Professional norms

Associated Press. "AP, other news organizations develop standards for use of artificial intelligence in newsrooms." August 16, 2023. [https://www.ap.org/media-center/ap-in-the-news/2023/ap-other-news-organizations-develop-standards-for-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-newsrooms/](https://www.ap.org/media-center/ap-in-the-news/2023/ap-other-news-organizations-develop-standards-for-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-newsrooms/)

The AP guidance emphasizes human responsibility and verification and restricts publication of AI-generated photo, video, or audio. It provides a professional example of an organization choosing stricter limits than a tool's technical capabilities.

## Source-use note

These sources are used to describe the shape and limitations of available mitigations. The ethical scenarios in Phase A are original hypotheticals grounded in the Task 6 artifact. No documented victim or real-world deepfake incident is used as the basis of the analysis.
