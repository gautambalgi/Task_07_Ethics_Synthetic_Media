# Synthetic Media Use and Governance Policy

## Proposed Policy for a University Communications Office

**Policy status:** Proposed governance document  
**Policy owner:** Director of University Communications  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective date if adopted:** October 1, 2026  
**Review cycle:** Every six months and after any material incident

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the creation, approval, publication, retention, and withdrawal of synthetic media by a university communications office. Its purpose is to permit limited uses that improve accessibility, translation, and production efficiency while protecting consent, factual accuracy, individual identity, institutional trust, and public safety.

The office will not treat disclosure as permission to create any form of synthetic media. A use must be truthful, necessary, proportionate, authorized, and reviewable in addition to being disclosed.

## 2. Organizational context

This policy is written for the central communications office of a mid-sized university. The office includes writers, designers, photographers, videographers, social-media managers, student employees, contractors, and communications leaders. It produces material for current and prospective students, faculty, staff, families, alumni, news media, and the public.

The office may be tempted to use synthetic media for narration, accessibility, multilingual content, orientation material, training, and production prototypes. At the same time, its work is often interpreted as the official voice of the institution. A synthetic message can therefore borrow institutional authority even when no accountable official personally delivered it. This policy responds to that risk.

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees, interns, student workers, volunteers, and contractors acting for the university communications office.
- Vendors that create or distribute content on behalf of the office.
- Public and internal audio, video, images, avatars, voiceovers, and materially altered media.
- Synthetic content created with university accounts, devices, data, budgets, branding, or distribution channels.
- Drafts and prototypes when they contain the identifiable voice, face, body, or mannerisms of a real person.

The policy does not prohibit ordinary technical corrections such as noise reduction, color correction, compression, captioning, or removal of a verbal pause when those changes do not create a false impression of what occurred. Staff must ask the policy owner when the distinction is uncertain.

## 4. Definitions

**Synthetic media:** Audio, video, imagery, or audiovisual material generated or materially altered by an artificial-intelligence system so that it depicts speech, appearance, action, or an event that was not captured in that form.

**Synthetic voice:** Speech generated by a model, including generic text-to-speech voices and cloned or imitated voices.

**Synthetic likeness:** A generated or materially altered representation that is reasonably identifiable as a particular person.

**Generic preset:** A provider-supplied voice or avatar that is not intentionally modeled on an identifiable individual.

**Material alteration:** A change that could affect a reasonable audience member's understanding of who spoke, what occurred, what was said, or whether an event was real.

**Disclosure:** Clear language presented to the audience explaining that content is AI-generated or materially altered.

**Provenance record:** Information documenting the source, creator, tools, model or preset, dates, edits, approvals, and distribution history of an asset.

**Content Credentials:** Tamper-evident provenance information implemented through the C2PA standard or a compatible successor.

**High-impact communication:** A message involving safety, emergencies, admissions or financial-aid decisions, employment, discipline, health, legal rights, official endorsements, elections, or another matter where a recipient may act because of perceived personal or institutional authority.

## 5. Governing principles

All approved uses must satisfy the following principles:

1. **Truthfulness:** Every factual claim must be verified through the same or a stronger process used for comparable non-synthetic communication.
2. **Accountability:** A named employee remains responsible for the content. The synthetic speaker is never the accountable author.
3. **Consent:** An identifiable person's voice or likeness may be used only through the workflow in Section 9.
4. **Transparency:** Audiences must be told when realistic communication is synthetic or materially altered.
5. **Minimum necessary use:** The office must prefer a less risky method when it can meet the same communication need.
6. **Human review:** No public synthetic-media asset may be published through an entirely automated workflow.
7. **Refusal:** The office must decline uses whose risks cannot be reduced to an acceptable level.

## 6. Permitted uses

The following uses are permitted after the required review:

- Generic synthetic narration for accessibility or translation when a human recording is impractical and the translation has been reviewed by a qualified person.
- Generic narration for routine instructional or orientation material that does not communicate a high-impact decision.
- Clearly fictional or stylized creative material that cannot reasonably be mistaken for a real event or real speaker.
- Internal prototypes using generic presets, provided they are marked as drafts and are not distributed outside the authorized project team.
- Restoration of intelligibility in an existing authorized recording, such as removing noise or improving clarity, when the edit does not change the speaker's words, apparent emotion, or meaning.
- A synthetic representation of a consenting person for a defined accessibility purpose when no prohibited-use rule applies and all enhanced approvals are obtained.

Permission is limited to the approved script, asset, channels, audience, and time period. Reuse for a new purpose requires a new review.

## 7. Uses requiring enhanced review

The following uses are not automatically prohibited, but require written approval from the Communications Director, the Privacy or Legal Office, and any represented individual:

- Any use of an identifiable person's synthetic voice or likeness.
- Paid advertising or fundraising communication containing realistic synthetic media.
- Content involving minors.
- Public distribution expected to reach more than one platform or to be translated into multiple languages.
- Content on health, campus security, financial aid, admissions procedures, or legal obligations that is informational but does not communicate an individual decision.
- A vendor-operated system that stores a person's voice, face, biometric information, or identity-linked training material.

Reviewers may impose additional restrictions, require a non-synthetic alternative, or refuse the project.

## 8. Prohibited uses

The office will not create, approve, fund, or distribute synthetic media that:

- Makes a person appear to say or do something without valid written consent.
- Uses the voice or likeness of a university leader to simulate a personal endorsement, apology, statement of belief, or other message dependent on that person's authority.
- Communicates or simulates an emergency alert, evacuation instruction, active-threat message, public-safety order, or crisis statement.
- Communicates an individualized admissions, financial-aid, employment, disciplinary, medical, or legal decision.
- Fabricates an event, quotation, testimonial, interview, news report, research result, or documentary record.
- Is materially false, misleading, defamatory, harassing, discriminatory, exploitative, or intended to manipulate an audience.
- Creates non-consensual intimate or sexualized material.
- Removes, obscures, or contradicts a required disclosure or provenance marker.
- Uses a deceased person's likeness unless expressly authorized through applicable rights holders and approved by the Legal Office, and even then only for a non-deceptive historical or educational purpose.
- Is designed to evade platform rules, detection systems, or institutional review.
- Uses confidential, student-record, personnel, medical, biometric, or other restricted data in an external generation service without documented authorization.
- Cannot retain an accountable human owner, source record, and approval history.

There is no exception to a prohibited use based solely on disclosure, urgency, low cost, or seniority of the requester.

## 9. Consent workflow

Before creating a synthetic representation of an identifiable person, the project owner must complete all of the following steps:

1. **Explain the proposed use.** Provide the person with the script or content category, tool or vendor, distribution channels, intended audience, expected duration, and whether the asset may be edited, translated, excerpted, or reused.
2. **Describe foreseeable risks.** Explain that synthetic media can be copied, altered, reposted, or separated from its disclosure and that complete removal from the internet cannot be guaranteed.
3. **Obtain written consent.** Consent must be affirmative, specific, voluntary, and separate from a general appearance release. Silence, employment status, participation in an event, or availability of public recordings does not constitute consent.
4. **Set boundaries.** The consent record must state the approved words or subject, channels, geographic scope if relevant, expiration date, and prohibited reuse.
5. **Provide review.** The represented person must be allowed to review the final asset before first publication.
6. **Record approval.** The project owner must store the consent and final approval with the asset's provenance record.
7. **Support revocation.** Revocation stops new publication and new reuse. The office must remove content from channels it controls when reasonably possible. The consent form must explain that previously downloaded or independently reposted copies may remain outside the university's control.

Consent expires after 12 months unless the form specifies a shorter period. Extension requires renewed written consent.

## 10. Factual verification and script control

Every synthetic-media script must have an identified author and fact-checker. The author may not serve as the only fact-checker for high-impact information.

The approved script must be retained. After generation, a reviewer must compare the output against the approved script for omissions, inserted words, pronunciation errors, altered emphasis, translation errors, and misleading tone. If a free-tier limit or technical constraint changes the script, the difference must be recorded and the changed version must be reviewed as a new output.

No reviewer may infer truth from realistic delivery, a detector score, a provider's safety label, or the presence of Content Credentials. Truth must be established from the underlying sources.

## 11. Disclosure standards

All realistic synthetic media must use plain language that an ordinary audience can understand. "Digitally enhanced" is insufficient when a voice, face, action, or scene was generated.

### Audio

- State within the first five seconds: "This audio uses an AI-generated voice."
- Repeat the disclosure at the end.
- Include the disclosure in the title, description, transcript, and downloadable file name.
- For content likely to be excerpted, include an additional brief identifier at reasonable intervals.

### Video

- Display "AI-generated voice," "AI-generated presenter," or another precise label at the beginning.
- Keep a readable disclosure visible throughout realistic synthetic segments.
- Include a spoken disclosure when audio is synthetic.
- Repeat the disclosure in captions, the description, and associated social posts.

### Images

- Place a visible disclosure adjacent to the image or within it when the image could be mistaken for a real event or person.
- Preserve disclosure in captions, alt text when appropriate, and downloadable file names.

### Platform controls

The uploader must also use every applicable platform disclosure field. A platform label supplements rather than replaces the university's own disclosure.

## 12. Provenance, records, and retention

The project owner must maintain a provenance record containing:

- Project name, purpose, audience, and accountable owner.
- Approved script and factual sources.
- Tool, provider, model, voice, avatar, and relevant settings.
- Creation and revision dates.
- Consent records and approval decisions.
- Original input, final output, and a cryptographic checksum of the approved final file.
- Disclosure language and screenshots or records of publication.
- Distribution channels and dates.
- Known incidents, corrections, withdrawal requests, and final disposition.

When supported by the workflow, the office must attach valid C2PA Content Credentials or a compatible form of tamper-evident provenance. Staff must not describe provenance as proof that the message is true. If a platform strips metadata, the internal record remains authoritative and the public verification page should identify the official version.

Records must be retained according to the university's records schedule and for no less than three years after the final publication date unless Legal or Records Management requires a longer period.

## 13. Review and approval

### Level 1 low risk

Examples: an internal prototype using a generic preset or a clearly fictional image.  
**Required approval:** Project owner and communications manager.

### Level 2 moderate risk

Examples: public generic narration, translated orientation content, or accessibility material.  
**Required approval:** Project owner, communications manager, and Communications Director. A qualified language or accessibility reviewer must approve relevant content.

### Level 3 high risk

Examples: identifiable likeness, paid promotion, content involving minors, or informational content near a high-impact domain.  
**Required approval:** Project owner, Communications Director, Privacy or Legal Office, represented person, and relevant subject-matter owner.

### Prohibited

A prohibited use has no approval path. Requests must be refused and documented.

Approvals must be written and completed before public distribution. A deadline does not authorize bypassing review.

## 14. Vendor and tool requirements

Before using an external system, the project owner and appropriate technology or privacy reviewer must assess:

- Whether inputs or outputs are retained or used for model training.
- Whether the vendor permits deletion and export of project data.
- Whether the system supports access controls, logs, watermarking, or Content Credentials.
- Whether licensing permits the intended institutional use.
- Whether voices or likenesses were lawfully provided.
- Whether the service can meet university privacy, security, accessibility, and records requirements.

Staff may use only approved accounts and services. Personal accounts may not be used for identifiable likenesses, confidential information, or official publication workflows.

## 15. Detection and verification

Automated detectors may be used for screening, triage, or investigation. They may not serve as the sole basis for:

- Declaring content authentic or synthetic.
- Accusing a person of misconduct.
- Approving publication.
- Closing an incident investigation.

Verification should combine available provenance, source confirmation, comparison with an official copy, technical inspection, contextual evidence, and human judgment. Conflicting detector outputs must be preserved in the incident or project record rather than averaged into a single unsupported conclusion.

## 16. Incident response

Anyone who discovers that university synthetic media is mislabeled, altered, misattributed, used outside its consent, or distributed to an unintended audience must immediately notify the Communications Director and Information Security contact.

The response team must:

1. Preserve the original, altered copy, URLs, timestamps, platform notices, approvals, logs, and consent records.
2. Pause scheduled distribution and remove the university-controlled copy when continued publication could increase harm.
3. Determine whether safety, privacy, legal rights, academic records, finances, or an identifiable person are affected.
4. Notify the represented person and relevant university offices when appropriate.
5. Contact platforms or distributors with a correction, provenance evidence, or removal request.
6. Publish a correction through authenticated university channels when audiences may act on the false or altered content.
7. Document decisions, residual risk, and unresolved copies.
8. Complete a root-cause review and corrective-action plan within 10 business days when feasible.

Emergency response, law-enforcement contact, and legally required notifications must follow applicable university procedures and law.

## 17. Refusal standard

The office must refuse a project when any of the following is true:

- Consent is absent, ambiguous, coerced, expired, or broader than the person's reasonable understanding.
- The content depends on an audience believing that a particular person personally spoke.
- Disclosure cannot remain clear in the expected format and distribution path.
- The content concerns an emergency or individualized high-impact decision.
- The factual basis cannot be verified.
- The tool's data practices, licensing, or security cannot be established.
- The office cannot maintain an accountable owner and provenance record.
- The probable harm outweighs the communication benefit.

When uncertain, staff must pause the project and escalate it. They are not required to complete a synthetic-media request merely because the technology is available.

## 18. Training, audit, and enforcement

Staff with access to synthetic-media tools must receive annual training on this policy, consent, disclosure, data handling, misinformation risk, and incident reporting.

The policy owner will conduct a semiannual audit of approved projects, consent records, disclosure compliance, vendor use, and open incidents. The audit will sample published assets to determine whether labels and provenance survive common distribution paths.

Violations may result in removal of tool access, withdrawal of content, retraining, vendor termination, or disciplinary action under applicable university procedures. Good-faith reporting of an error or policy concern must not be discouraged.

## 19. Policy review

The Communications Director, Privacy or Legal Office, Information Security, Accessibility Office, and Records Management will review this policy every six months and after any material incident. Reviewers will consider changes in generation quality, detection reliability, Content Credentials adoption, platform requirements, law, vendor practices, and audience understanding.

## 20. Human accountability statement

Every asset approved under this policy must identify a human owner. The office may delegate narration to a synthetic system, but it may not delegate responsibility for truth, consent, disclosure, or consequences.
